7. **Commands.** `fc timing knobs show | check | update | rollback [N] | reset [backend] | export-defaults`.
8. **Documentation.** The user guide, architecture, the HTTP guide, the phase README and changelog, and the research note (factory derivation, learning curve, cost).

Beyond the plan: the per-signature summaries are consumed as tier 1 once history is pruned, and the checked-in `*.example.yaml` files are tested equal to the factory code. Measured: loading the files takes 3.2 ms (target 20 ms), a 300-run fit 0.02 s of CPU (3 s), a forecast event 0.02 ms (5 ms). On the synthetic second-mode history the calibration brings H3's start-of-run error from 9.5% to about 4% after 16 completed runs, with the 80% range covering 0.84; size offsets and knob effects change it by about a point either way (see the research note). The owner-host validation (the learning curve on real runs, and one real H3 and one real Draw Things job compared with their actual ends) is pending and does not block this milestone. Known limits: a text-valued run knob is not recorded in the ring, so it cannot be learned; the first runs of a pipeline the factory only borrows (`i2av`, `i2i`, `t2v`) are priced with a wide range until a checkpoint helps; the unit of independence is the execution, so a host that always runs one job at a time and one size learns slopes slowly by design.

## Goal

Put the forecast's editable tunables and learned calibration under `data/timing-data-analysis/`, write bounded provisional estimates as runs complete, keep the owner's hand edits, and use factory defaults wherever a value has not been learned. Validating later changes against unseen executions, and searching the tunables, are [Milestone 15](milestone-15-timing-gate-and-search.md). Factory fallback values and hard validation bounds remain in code; operational safety floors are file tunables that the automatic search cannot change. **Tunables** are estimator decisions (the pause factor, how fast old evidence fades, how widely an unseen setting widens the range). **Calibration** is host measurement (step time by backend, pipeline, run knobs, and output width and height; stage durations; trends; spreads). The calibration files are updated by Framecontrol, with the owner's hand edits kept; `tuning.yaml` is written only by the owner and by `export-defaults` until Milestone 15.

## Evidence

**What Milestone 11 plans, and where it falls short for this.**

- Its thresholds are "provisional constants in `fit.py`" and in `features.py`, so a change is a code change and a release, and nothing records what was tried.
- It fits on the retained window of 14 days by default, and pruning deletes the rows with their execution. What one host learned about its own H3 and Draw Things settings is forgotten every two weeks, and the real evidence behind [Milestone 10](milestone-10-stage-timing.md#evidence) is already nearly gone ([Milestone 11's decisions](milestone-11-time-estimation.md#decisions-for-the-owner)).
- The step time grows with size, frames, and the run's knobs, and Milestone 11 fits only log pixels, log frames, a reference count, and the knobs that vary in the retained window. A knob the window never varied is not extrapolated, so the forecast drops to `live_only` the first time a known knob takes another value, although the same host may have timed it a month before.
- The original M11 fallback waited for two iterative-stage readings. M11 has landed and publishes at `JobStarted` from an exact factory point or a broad heuristic when no fit qualifies. M14 must preserve that first forecast and make its source clear while moving its editable numbers into the knob model.

**What a run records, and so what the knobs can depend on** (`timing_workload` in the backends):

| Backend | Workload keys |
|---------|---------------|
| H3 | `pipeline`, `width`, `height`, `pixels`, `frames`, `steps`, `seconds`, `references` (by kind) and `reference_count`, `render_width`, `render_height`, `denoise_reuse`, `dit_layers`, `core_reuse`, `token_reduction`, `ssd_streaming`, `int8_row_fc2`, `reference_rope`, `reference_image_size`, plus `color_corrects` or nondefault `compatibility` where applicable |
| Draw Things | `width`, `height`, `pixels`, `frames`, `steps`, `mode`, `guidance_scale`, `strength`, `shift`, `color_corrects` where applicable, and the model in the environment |

The M10 evidence already shows the dependence this milestone is for: per-step time at 832×448 was about 21 s for 5 s clips, 45 s for 8 s, and 65 s for 10 s, so it grows faster than the clip length, and H3's setup, tail, and Framecontrol finishing change with color correction (1 to 2 s against 38 to 46 s).

[E0053's live Draw Things measurement](../research/time-estimation.md#evidence) adds a narrow, model-specific calibration point: two 384×768, 33-frame, 25-step `i2v` runs averaged 13.33 and 13.95 s per step, with about 8 s of setup, 5 s of child finishing, and 8 s of Framecontrol finishing with color correction. They are one execution, so they cannot establish a size or frame-count slope or the between-execution spread. Its stored workload says 17 frames and 40 steps because the old timing hook missed per-run CLI overrides; the output and child counter say 33 and 25. The fit and replay loader now reconcile this old row. The future ring import must use those reconciled features too.

## Approach

**Files** (all under `data/timing-data-analysis/`, which `.gitignore` already treats as personal data; `ProjectPaths` gains `timing_data`, so no module holds a path of its own):

| File | Holds | Written by |
|------|-------|------------|
| `tuning.yaml` | The tunables, shared by both backends | the owner (the search of Milestone 15 writes it later) |
| `draw-things-cli.yaml`, `h3-metal-cli.yaml` | One backend's calibration, with a section per pipeline | the updater and the owner |
| `.machine/ring.jsonl` | Up to 300 complete runs per backend and pipeline of bounded numeric timing evidence, fixed labels, and opaque model identifiers | the updater |
| `.machine/written.json` | The last written digests, values, pins, and update counters, including each group's checkpoint count and last provisional fit | the updater |
| `.machine/pending.json`, `.machine/updates.log`, `.machine/backups/`, `.machine/lock` | Crash recovery for a multi-file update, its outcome, the newest 20 timestamped copies of each file, and the lock | the updater |

Milestone 15 adds `.machine/validated.json`, the last held-out-approved calibration. Until then the baseline is the factory.

A file carries a `schema` version and is validated before use. A known older schema is migrated under the same backup and recovery rules; a newer schema is read-only to this version of Framecontrol. `tuning.example.yaml`, `draw-things-cli.example.yaml`, and `h3-metal-cli.example.yaml` are factory exports, checked in by the `!data/**/*example*` rule, and a test keeps them equal to the factory code.

**Factory defaults** are code: `jobs/timing/factory.py`, which today holds the two landed measured points, grows to build a `Tunables` and a `Calibration` for each supported backend and pipeline. The research note identifies measured values and labels unmeasured scaling assumptions separately. The M10 evidence supports stage durations, H3 frame scaling over the observed clip lengths, Draw Things step time and within-stage rise, and Framecontrol finishing times. It does not establish a size exponent: the initial pixel exponent is an explicit, broad prior, never presented as measured. The factory records the workload and build of each measured baseline. A missing file or key uses that key's factory value; an invalid individual value is ignored without losing valid siblings. When a whole file is malformed or from a newer schema, use the newest valid compatible backup, then the factory if none exists, and leave the problematic file untouched until `check`, `rollback`, or an explicit repair resolves it. The worker and front ends never raise for a knob-file error.
The Draw Things factory must keep the earlier 40-step point and E0053's 25-step point tied to their model, size, frame count, and color policy; neither point justifies one backend-wide step time.
An E0053-specific factory point has been landed ahead of this milestone in `jobs/timing/factory.py` and is read by the current estimator only when no history tier qualifies. It prices that exact workload and executable build at 13.6710 s per later step, with a 0.99758 whole-stage factor, a 0.205 stage slope, and a broad 0.25 log spread; it does not create or load the planned knob files. This milestone carries that measured point into its full calibration format and keep its exact-match guard until independent workloads establish scaling coefficients.
An [E0051/E0054 H3 factory point](../research/time-estimation.md#evidence) has also landed for the exact overridden `r2av` workload: 15 requested seconds became 362 effective frames, with 30 steps, three image references, and one executable build. It uses 138.6683 s per later step, a 0.99334 whole-stage factor, the observed denoise and FFmpeg stages, a first-gap ratio of 0.798, a 0.1685 step slope, and the same broad 0.25 log spread. The full calibration model must carry the effective frames and per-run override, not train this point as 360 frames or as a profile default.

**The model** (`jobs/timing/calibrate.py`, pure, `core` and `numpy` only). Per backend and pipeline, the step time of the iterative stage is `exp(b0 + b_pixels log pixels + b_frames log frames + b_aspect log aspect + b_render log render pixels + b_refs log(1 + references) + the effect of each run knob + the size offset)`:

- **Width and height.** A continuous power law over pixels and aspect applies to any size. Each exact `WxH` also gets an offset, the mean residual of its runs shrunk toward zero by `n / (n + k)` (`k` is the tunable `size_offset_shrinkage`, and `n` is execution-weighted evidence so many runs in one job do not impersonate independent measurements). A size used often sharpens and a new size follows the curve. H3's render size enters on its own. Aspect enters only where independent data varies it.
- **Run knobs.** Each work-changing knob has one learned effect (a number as its log slope, a flag or a value as an indicator); seed, timeout, requested seconds when effective frames are known, and reference-pair name are excluded as in M11. A slope is fit only when that knob varies across independent executions after accounting for correlated settings. The file records observed values by backend, pipeline, and model identity. A value never seen and different from the factory baseline widens the range by `unseen_knob_inflation` and puts its name in `unseen` in the forecast's basis.
- **Other stages** (setup, decode, FFmpeg, Framecontrol finishing, including the color-correction one) carry their own log-linear fit on frames and pixels and the residual spread. The file also holds the within-stage trend (kept only where its interval excludes zero, as in M11), the first-gap ratio, the run and execution spreads, and a bounded top 40 of per-signature summaries (count, median stage times, last seen) so tier 1 of M11 survives pruning. Persist only sanitized workload fields and opaque model fingerprints in those summaries.

**Evidence kept.** Raw timing rows still follow `history_retention_days`. The ring is a second, bounded copy of complete, live runs, capped per backend and pipeline so a busy pipeline cannot evict a rare one's evidence, made from the same reconciled `HistoryRun` that M11 fits (including E0053's corrected frames and steps), never from the stale raw workload. It records execution and run numbers, backend and pipeline labels from fixed enums, numeric workload and build facts, fixed stage and unit labels, durations, and at most 120 numeric marks per run. An unknown stage is represented by the fixed `other` label with its measured duration. A model is an opaque stable fingerprint (a truncated SHA-256 of its identity, used only for equality and kept local in the ignored `data/` tree); no model filename, arbitrary string knob, prompt, path, log line, or output name is persisted. A strict recursive key and type whitelist rejects an entry containing anything else. Deduplicate ring and retained-history rows by execution and run before fitting. Imported `log` rows may assist M11's ordinary forecast but do not enter the ring or train calibration terms requiring missing settings. The ring is the replay source after database pruning (Milestone 15's gate reads it). Pruning cannot erase accepted calibration already in the files.

**The update** (`jobs/timing/calibrate.py` for the fit and `services/timing_knobs.py`, `services/knob_updater.py` for the files and the worker side). After each complete run, off the event loop and one update at a time (a run that finishes during one is coalesced into the next). Count complete runs per backend and pipeline, including executions with just one run:

1. Take the lock, recover any interrupted update, load the files, and detect pins (below). If a file is malformed or newer than the supported schema, forecast from its last valid backup but suspend automatic writes to that file.
2. Append the normalized run to the ring, dropping a duplicate `(execution, run)` and the oldest of its group beyond 300. Backfill from retained complete live rows once on a new installation, in chronological order through the same checkpoints, using the same normalization and deduplication.
3. At completed-run counts **1, 2, 4, 8, 16, and 32**, write a **provisional** calibration from the evidence then available, even without a held-out execution. With only one execution, adjust only a local measured point or intercept, not a size, frame, aspect, or knob slope; open each term only when independent executions vary it. Clamp changes, validate bounds, and widen the forecast range for provisional values. The baseline is the factory defaults. Persist the checkpoint count and last provisional fit in `.machine/written.json`. A one-run job counts as one independent execution.
4. Write each changed file atomically (temporary file in the same directory, flush, then rename) with a backup and a `pending.json` transaction record that names the prior file set. Update `written.json` last; on restart, complete or roll back an interrupted transaction before comparing digests for hand edits. Keep the newest 20 backups of each file and a transaction manifest for `rollback [N]`. An edit that appears between read and write aborts the update, so it is reconsidered as a possible pin instead of overwritten.

After the 32-run checkpoint of a backend and pipeline, provisional learning is frozen and every later run still enters the ring until [Milestone 15](milestone-15-timing-gate-and-search.md) adds the held-out gate that can validate a further change. The forecast keeps using the last provisional calibration, labeled as such.

**Scheduling.** The update runs on a background thread at low priority, so it cannot slow the event path or disturb a run's timing. It starts when the queue is idle or in the cooldown after a run, not while a run is generating, and updates missed meanwhile coalesce into the next. A single update stays within the CPU target below.

**The tunables** (`tuning.yaml`; factory values are provisional constants in `factory.py`, pinned by tests, and each has hard bounds in `knobs.py`). Milestone 15 adds the grid the search may move each one on.

| Tunable | Factory | Meaning |
|---------|---------|---------|
| `pause_factor` | 3.0 | A step this many times the median is a pause |
| `size_offset_shrinkage` | 3 | Runs a size needs before its offset is half trusted |
| `prior_weight_cap` | 12 | Most pseudo-runs the previous calibration weighs |
| `half_life_runs` | 60 | How fast old evidence fades |
| `unseen_knob_inflation` | 1.5 | Range widening for a knob value never seen |
| `rho` | 0.5 | Share of the speed factor common to one execution, until spreads are measured |
| `slope_level` | 0.95 | Interval a within-stage slope must exclude zero at |
| `factory_spread` | 0.25 | The wider run spread used from factory defaults |
| Tier counts (3 runs from 2 executions; 6 from 3), the 80% range, the 2 s publish interval, the 200 ms fit and 5 ms event budgets, the ring size, the backup count | as M11 | Safety floors and budgets. A hand edit changes them; no automatic process does |

**Hand edits** use digest detection, as the owner chose: a file is rewritten whole, and `written.json` keeps the exact file digest and the typed values the updater last wrote. A changed digest triggers a per-key comparison. A changed valid value is **pinned**: the updater keeps it, fits the remaining terms around it, and never searches it. A deleted key removes its pin and returns to automatic. A comment-only or `notes` change does not pin a number. The `evidence` block and `updated_at` are read-only; edits to them are ignored with a warning. Keep the bounded `notes:` block through a machine rewrite, and retain the earlier text in backups. A malformed file is not silently treated as a set of deletions or pins. `show` and `check` expose every pin and the file or backup from which each effective value came.

**The forecast.** Preserve M11's first publication at `JobStarted` when a valid planned workload can be priced. Exact measured factory points keep their narrow match; other supported workloads use a broad factory prior derived from M11's heuristic, with an explicit extrapolation label and a wide range. A sparse current-build history still takes precedence over a scaled older-build anchor as M11 specifies. The basis gains `knobs` (`factory`, `provisional`, or `pinned` when a pin touched the figure; Milestone 15 adds `learned`), `knobs_updated_at`, and unseen knob names. The tier continues to distinguish an exact `factory` point, a history fit, and a broad `heuristic` extrapolation; `knobs` names the source of its values. The live update takes over as steps arrive. If the workload is missing facts needed to price it, preserve `live_only` rather than fabricate a number. The published object, floor rule, and publishing rule are otherwise as in M11.

Rejected: sufficient statistics (`XᵀX`, `Xᵀy` kept per backend: exact, but opaque, a second numeric format to keep in step with the YAML, and the gate still needs a ring); a YAML-only incremental update with no ring (nothing to replay); a per-key `pinned` flag in one file (PyYAML drops the owner's comments on every machine rewrite); a calibration sweep of deliberate runs (GPU hours for knowledge passive learning supplies).

## Scope

In scope:

- The knob model and factory in `jobs/timing/`: `knobs.py` (`Tunables`, `Calibration`, their validation and bounds), `factory.py`, and `calibrate.py` (the fit, size offsets, shrinkage, clamping). They import only `core` and `numpy`.
- In `services/`: `timing_knobs.py` (load, validation, digests and pins, atomic writes, the lock, backups, the ring) and `knob_updater.py` (the worker-side hook, scheduling, coalescing).
- `ProjectPaths.timing_data`, the `fc timing knobs` commands, and `knobs` in the forecast basis on the HTTP, gRPC, and MCP surfaces and in the TUI's and the CLI's forecast text.
- The refactoring of Milestone 11's landed code (planned change 2).
- The synthetic data set's second mode (truth set away from the factory) and the tests below.

Out of scope: queue-wide prediction; recommending knobs for a job; a calibration sweep of deliberate runs; thermal, GPU, or host-load modeling (the ring records the start load, so a later milestone can test it); a second host (one machine only, by owner decision); copying logs or per-run rows beyond the numbers the ring holds; changing generation.

## Evaluation

- **Synthetic data, second mode.** The existing seeded generator (at least 2000 runs, at least 20 signatures, both backends) writes a truth whose step times, stage durations, and size and knob effects are set 30% away from the factory values, and varies what the model does not assume (a rebuilt executable, a heavy-tailed run factor, drift, a pause, a falling step time). An offline report starts from no files, replays runs in time order through the real update schedule, and measures M11's checkpoints. A smaller deterministic fixture in `make check` verifies update, pin, and recovery behavior without running the entire learning curve on every check.
- **Metrics:** M11's (median and 90th-percentile absolute percentage error of remaining time, bias, 80% coverage, time to the first number, share moving back), taken at 0, 10, 25, 50, 75, and 90% of each run, by completed runs of the pipeline (0, 1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32, and after 32 while learning is frozen) and for a size seen 0, 1, and 5 times. Report execution counts and uncertainty intervals; do not treat runs from one execution as independent samples.
- **Baselines:** the factory knobs with no files, and the calibration with the size offsets and knob effects switched off, so each addition is measured.
- **Real data** is the owner's own host, measured once like M11's. Report accuracy and coverage to the owner; neither synthetic nor real accuracy has a CI or completion threshold. Milestone 15 adds the held-out gate for changes after the provisional checkpoints.

## Acceptance criteria

Required behavior, tested in CI with deterministic fixtures:

- With no files, each supported backend and pipeline whose workload is priceable gets a forecast at `JobStarted` from an exact factory point or a broad heuristic prior, with the correct tier and `knobs` source. Its error and coverage are reported, not gated.
- At counts 1, 2, 4, 8, 16, and 32, the updater may write bounded provisional calibration, including from single-run executions. A single execution cannot establish slopes across size, frames, aspect, or knobs. Provisional values and their widened range are named in the basis. After the 32-run checkpoint of a backend and pipeline the calibration does not change until Milestone 15, and every later run still enters the ring.
- A size never seen uses the power law, while a seen size may receive a shrunk offset. An unseen work-changing knob value widens the range and is named in `unseen`; after it has been observed in independent executions, the fitting logic may learn its effect. Report whether size offsets and knob effects improve measured error; no accuracy threshold gates CI or completion.
- A run 10 times slower than predicted moves no coefficient beyond the clamp. A candidate with invalid values is rejected; the ring retains the run, the files and digests stay, and `updates.log` explains why.
- Writes are atomic (a failure injected between the temporary file and rename leaves the old file), concurrent updates serialize on the lock, transaction recovery never mistakes a machine write for a pin, rollback restores a backup, and only the newest 20 backups per file remain. A corrupt or newer-schema file uses the last valid compatible backup, then factory defaults, without being overwritten automatically; an invalid key uses the factory value for that key while valid siblings remain. A missing directory starts from factory defaults. None of these raises in the worker or a front end.
- A value the test edits by hand is pinned across updates and its terms are fitted around it; deleting the key unpins it; an edit to `evidence` is ignored with a warning.
- The ring holds at most 300 runs per backend and pipeline, holds no run twice, and a test asserts every key of an entry is on the whitelist (no prompt, path, or log text). Pruning the database does not remove what the files learned, and the forecast after pruning still uses them.
- After the refactoring step (planned change 2) and before anything else changes, every Milestone 11 test passes with the factory pair and the synthetic replay's published forecasts are identical to those before it.
- Benchmark file load, a 300-run fit, and per-event forecast cost against targets of 20 ms, 3 s of CPU, and 5 ms respectively; report measured values. The updater runs on a low-priority thread, starts only while the queue is idle or cooling down, and never delays the worker's event path.
- `tests/test_architecture.py` fails if `tui/`, `cli/`, or `mcp_server/` imports `jobs/timing/knobs.py`, `factory.py`, `calibrate.py`, or `services/knob_updater.py`; `cli/timing_app.py` may call the public `services/timing_knobs.py` API. No dependency is added, and `make check` passes.

Owner host validation, recorded in `docs/research/time-estimation.md` and not blocking completion: the learning curve on the real runs the host completes after the knobs go live (the first number's error with factory defaults, and at provisional counts 1, 2, 4, 8, 16, and 32), and one real H3 job and one real Draw Things job forecast from the files compared with the actual end.

## Decisions for the owner

- **The knobs are both tunables and calibration** (owner decision, 2026-10-16). Tunables are decisions and calibration is measurement, and both are files, and the updater writes the calibration.
- **The files are the durable memory** (owner decision, 2026-10-16), with a bounded numbers-only ring beside them. This supersedes, for those numbers only, [Milestone 10's](milestone-10-stage-timing.md#decisions-for-the-owner) "timing data follows history retention" and its rejection of a separate store: raw rows, stages, and marks still follow `history_retention_days`, and the ring carries nothing a prompt, path, or log could.
- **One file per backend with the pipelines inside, plus one tuning file** (owner decision, 2026-10-16).
- **Automatic updates with a held-out gate after provisional learning** (owner decision, 2026-10-16): write bounded provisional calibration at 1, 2, 4, 8, 16, and 32 completed runs, including single-run jobs; later validated changes require a comparison on independent executions that occurred after the provisional fit. This supersedes this milestone's earlier statement that every write is replay-gated. Every write keeps a backup, and `rollback` restores an earlier transaction. The gate is Milestone 15 (see the split decision below).
- **The tunables are searched too** (owner decision, 2026-10-16, against the recommendation to search calibration only), in [Milestone 15](milestone-15-timing-gate-and-search.md#decisions-for-the-owner). This milestone only reads `tuning.yaml`.
- **Split into Milestones 14 and 15** (owner decision, 2026-10-18): this milestone ships the seam, factory, files, provisional learning, pins, backups, and commands; the held-out gate and the tunables search follow in Milestone 15, so the riskier statistics get their own evidence.
- **The updater runs at low priority, when the queue is idle or cooling down** (owner decision, 2026-10-18), so learning does not disturb the timing it measures; missed updates coalesce.
- **The ring is capped per backend and pipeline** (owner decision, 2026-10-18), so a busy pipeline cannot evict a rare one's evidence.
- **Model identity is a truncated SHA-256 fingerprint** (design decision, 2026-10-18), local and ignored by git, used only for equality.
- **Hand edits are detected by digest** (owner decision, 2026-10-16). Rejected: layered override files, which keep comments and make pinning explicit, and a per-key flag in one file. The cost is that PyYAML drops comments on a machine rewrite, held down by the `notes` block and the backups.
- **Width and height as a power law plus per-size offsets** (owner decision, 2026-10-16).
- **A forecast at the first moment** (owner decision, 2026-10-16), with a wide range where extrapolated, superseding [Milestone 11's](milestone-11-time-estimation.md#approach) "no number until the iterative stage has 2 readings" where the factory or files can price the run. M11 now publishes at `JobStarted`; its tier remains `factory` for an exact point and `heuristic` for a scaled prior, while the added `knobs` field names the value source.
- **Report accuracy without a CI threshold** (owner decision, 2026-10-16): measure synthetic and owner-host accuracy and coverage and report them to the owner. Functionality and safety tests still pass `make check`; the automatic updater's held-out acceptance rule remains in force after provisional learning.
- **Last valid compatible values on file failure** (owner decision, 2026-10-16): a malformed or newer-schema file uses its last valid compatible backup when one exists, then the factory, without an automatic overwrite.
- **A bounded ring with opaque identities** (owner decision, 2026-10-16): fixed labels, numeric measurements, and stable model fingerprints identify work without persisting model filenames or arbitrary text.
- **One machine** (owner decision, 2026-10-16). The knobs describe this host and nothing keys them by host. A second Mac would train and be forecast by the first's numbers.
- **The persistence engine is a ring with prior shrinkage** (design decision, 2026-10-16), over sufficient statistics and a YAML-only update.
- **Milestone 11 is left untouched** (owner decision, 2026-10-16). Its document and build stay as planned, and this milestone's planned change 2 refactors its landed code onto the `Tunables` and `Calibration` seam, behavior-preserving and verified by identical replay output before any behavior changes.

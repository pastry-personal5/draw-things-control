# h3.c for Phase 4

Researched 2026-10-12 for [Phase 4, Milestone 03](../phase-4/milestone-03-h3-i2av.md), then updated 2026-10-13 for [Milestone 05](../phase-4/milestone-05-h3-r2av.md). Source: the [antirez/h3.c README](https://github.com/antirez/h3.c) and its [CLI entry point](https://github.com/antirez/h3.c/blob/main/main.c), checked against the local `contrib/backends/h3.c` checkout; the current [MiniMax H3 README](https://github.com/MiniMax-AI/MiniMax-H3) and `MiniMaxAI/MiniMax-H3` Hub listing were checked for Ref2VA model limits. The underlying model's documentation describes a broader set of modes; the plans target the h3.c executable's current interface.

- h3.c is an Apple Silicon MiniMax H3 inference engine. Its one-shot CLI takes `-d MODEL_DIR`, `-p PROMPT`, `-o OUTPUT`, dimensions, temporal controls, steps, seed, and conditioning flags. Without a prompt it enters an interactive session, which does not fit the existing run-boundary queue model.
- `--first-frame` selects first-frame conditioning. `--ref-image` uses a separate ordered-reference checkpoint and treats the image as a reference rather than the opening frame. The owner chose `--first-frame` for `i2av` chains.
- The output is MP4 with synchronized H.264 video and 32 kHz stereo AAC audio. FFmpeg and FFprobe must be available for media inputs and MP4 output; the executable also accepts `H3_FFMPEG` and `H3_FFPROBE` overrides.
- Output is 24 fps. Requested frame counts are rounded upward to `5 + 17*n` (for example, 23 becomes 39). Width and height must be multiples of 32; their product is capped at `768 * 1344` pixels. Short development clips work, while the released workflow targets roughly 4–15 seconds.
- The public CLI currently supports first/last-frame conditioning and ordered image, video, and audio references. These are different modes with different inputs; only first-frame `i2av` is planned for the initial integration.
- The local checkout contains `contrib/backends/h3.c/h3` and `contrib/backends/h3.c/MiniMax-H3`. The executable path is the owner's chosen integration path; the model snapshot location is the planned default to validate at implementation time.
- Verified on 2026-10-12 against the checked-in executable and `main.c`: `--seconds` parses a positive finite duration as `llround(seconds * 24)` frames, and `--seconds` with `--frames` is rejected as mutually exclusive. These CLI parsing rules do not require Framecontrol to select `--seconds`: `--frames` accepts an integer directly, and h3.c aligns that count upward to the next `5 + 17*n` value. The Codex execution sandbox reports `h3: no Metal device available` for `--info`, although the underlying host can run H3; a human performs the actual first-frame inference and MP4 inspection on that host.
- Updated 2026-10-14: Framecontrol validates the job's requested duration and frame count together, snaps the count to the nearest supported grid value, and passes that exact value through `--frames` alone. A 10-second request therefore passes `--frames 243`, avoiding a second conversion through floating-point seconds.
- Updated 2026-10-13: h3.c exposes optional internal render size, denoiser reuse, retained DiT layers, core reuse, token reduction, SSD weight streaming, row-FC2 quantization, released-reference RoPE, and ten slower compatibility paths. The supported ranges in the checked-in validator are render dimensions that are same-aspect and no larger than the output, denoiser reuse 1–3, layers 35–50, and core reuse 1–6. Denoiser reuse and core reuse cannot both exceed 1. `--use-int8-row-fc2` requires a Metal 4 GPU and cannot be combined with `--ssd-streaming` or `--use-slower-bf16-mlp`. The h3.c comments identify 1/50/1 as close-reference defaults, 2/45/4 as validated fast values, and 3/40/6 as aggressive values; the latter tuples describe separate reuse alternatives rather than valid simultaneous settings.
- Ref2VA takes an ordered list of `--ref-image`, `--ref-video`, `--ref-silent-video`, `--ref-video-audio VIDEO AUDIO`, and `--ref-audio` flags plus `--ref-image-size match|max`. Its local validator accepts at most 9 images, 3 videos, 3 audio inputs, and 12 references; audio needs an image or video reference. `--ref-silent-video` does not count as audio, while `--ref-video` and `--ref-video-audio` do. The upstream MiniMax H3 documentation adds a 2–15 second limit for each video/audio reference; h3.c caps total decoded reference audio at 15 seconds and truncates a video's soundtrack to the video frames it consumes. Video references with audio require at least 56 effective output frames. h3.c selects Ref2VA when references are present and rejects references combined with first/last frame anchors; a mixed Framechain plan must therefore make the Ref2VA run independent, then may pass its verified output frame only to a later FL2VA `i2av` run.

## Ref2VA prompt structure

Framechain passes the selected positive prompt unchanged. Organize an `r2av` prompt with these six sections, in order:

1. `subject_definitions`
2. `summary`
3. `retention_analysis`
4. `detailed_description`
5. `overall_soundscape`
6. `non_diegetic_music`

Reference labels are assigned in declared pair order, independently by kind: `<Picture 1>`, `<Video 1>`, `<Audio 1>`, and so on. A `video_audio` entry contributes one video and one audio label; its separate audio uses the next audio ordinal. The prompt should use labels for the pair selected by that run. Framechain records the selected pair and asset inventory but does not rewrite or interpret natural-language prompt text.

The owner approved adopting that source sRGB/BT.709-primaries convention as an engineering assumption for the characterized executable SHA-256 `79d4386f8f0825992f2204c643554fbba7c9c44fd7bfe54f1c6b2ba22308be9f`. The finisher now verifies the fingerprint and the child's separate H.264, stream, and container declarations before conversion. It converts under that convention, verifies BT.709 limited declarations in all three places, checks the full H3 audiovisual structure, and compares every native AAC packet hash and timestamp with the child. On conversion failure it attempts a metadata-only native `nclx 1/13/5` tag with unchanged video/audio packets and timestamps. On unknown fingerprint or conflicting child declarations it retains the child unchanged and omits an unsafe handoff. This decision is limited to the measured encode path and the agreed source convention; it is not new transfer or primaries measurement. Existing `i2av` MP4s remain useful for visual review but do not have a matching PPM/fingerprint capture. A human mixed-chain validation and numerical correction-cap characterization remain open.

The H3 correction transform is version 1 and operates on an explicit sRGB decode. Per run it caps median Oklab lightness movement at 0.03, contrast at 0.95–1.05, chroma gain at 0.92–1.08, hue turn at ±4°, and neutral-cast movement at 0.01. It reduces the entire transform when more than 2% of pixels would need gamut-edge mapping; if six reductions cannot meet the cap, that frame uses identity. A five-frame weighted smoother limits frame-to-frame jumps. `previous` targets the prepared input; `first` and `blend` add a smooth ramp toward the current i2av segment anchor. These are conservative engineering caps tested on synthetic dark and saturated patches and a synthetic H.264/AAC clip. They have not yet been judged against a same-fingerprint real `i2av` correction run; the human mixed-chain validation remains recommended release validation but is not a Milestone 06 completion gate.

With `regions: true`, the shared Apple Vision segmenter supplies person and skin masks. H3 plans people and background corrections separately when people occupy enough of the prepared input and at least half the run's frames; skin is treated separately when its mask also qualifies. Skin movement uses narrower caps: lightness ±0.015, chroma 0.96–1.04, hue ±2°, and cast 0.005. Smoothed and feathered masks blend each result into the frame. Absent or insufficient segmentation reports a whole-frame fallback. The same optional masks add per-region first-to-last, input-to-first, and anchor-to-last drift comparisons. Synthetic segmented H.264/AAC tests verify the regional branch, fallback, and native AAC packet identity; real Apple Vision output still needs human review in the mixed-chain validation.

A direct finisher trial on a private copy of the supplied 124-frame `r2av` child produced an 848,933-byte Rec.709 delivery with no warning and measured first-to-last Oklab lightness drift of −1.48 hundredths, contrast ratio 0.9822, chroma ratio 0.9592, and hue −1.88°. A second trial used that video's explicitly decoded first frame as a stand-in `i2av` input. Its 0.5-strength correction took 7.74 seconds in the standalone two-pass encoder, kept every AAC packet hash and timestamp, and wrote matching Rec.709 container, H.264 VUI, and FFprobe stream declarations. The integrated finisher succeeded without warnings, retained the 963,119-byte byte-identical raw child, the 848,933-byte uncorrected delivery, an 825,987-byte corrected main output, and a verified sRGB final-frame PNG handoff. This exercises the real H3 pixel and audio path but does not replace a real same-fingerprint `i2av` inference run or human review of its corrected colors.
